Purpose

A map of static structure

A Class Diagram shows the classes that matter, the data they own, the behavior they offer, and how those classes are related.

Multiplicity and navigability are constraints on that structure. Call order belongs on a sequence or activity diagram.

Purpose

Five questions

  1. 1What concepts exist?
  2. 2What information does each one own?
  3. 3What operations can it perform?
  4. 4How are the concepts related?
  5. 5How many instances may participate?

Association

Two different questions

Navigability

Which direction can be traversed? An open arrow answers that, and only when the direction matters.

Multiplicity

How many instances may participate? A number such as 0..* is not an arrow, and an arrow is not “many.”

Association

Replace the department string

Each employee works in exactly one department. A department has zero or many employees. The arrow says Employee can reach Department.

Shared aggregation

A hollow diamond on the whole

A tortoise may belong to a creep and still exist on its own. Use the hollow diamond only when that aggregate meaning is real.

Shared aggregation

A plain line is often more honest

  • Do not draw a hollow diamond just to say “has-a.”
  • Do not draw it just because a class holds a collection.
  • Do not draw it just because the part can exist alone.

UML leaves shared aggregation weak. Prefer a plain association when the team cannot agree what the diamond adds.

Composition

A filled diamond on the whole

One visitor center owns exactly one lobby, and one or more bathrooms. Each part belongs to that one center.

Composition

Ask before you fill the diamond

  1. Is the part owned by at most one whole at a time?
  2. Is the part’s identity meaningful inside that whole?
  3. Does the whole govern creation, transfer, or destruction?
  4. Would shared ownership contradict the domain?

“Delete the whole, delete the part” is only an intuition. A database cascade does not prove composition.

Read the marks, not the layout

  • Customer and Administrator specialize User.

Building the diagram

Eight passes, in this order

  1. 1. State the view and its scope.
  2. 2. Keep concepts that have identity and responsibility.
  3. 3. Assign information and behavior.
  4. 4. Start with plain associations.
  5. 5. Add multiplicity from the rules.
  6. 6. Add generalization only after the is-a test.
  7. 7. Add visibility when the view needs it.
  8. 8. Review ownership, both ends, and readability.

Review

Mistakes that show up early

  • Turning every noun into a class.
  • Drawing the database schema instead of the domain.
  • Inheriting only to reuse code.
  • Using a hollow diamond as decoration for “has-a.”
  • Using composition because a delete cascades.
  • Reading 0..* as a navigable arrow.
  • Filling classes with getters and setters.
  • Guessing a multiplicity the requirements never stated.

Review

Before you keep the diagram

  • The view and abstraction level are stated.
  • Names are singular domain nouns.
  • Every generalization passes the is-a test.
  • Important classes are not hidden as id attributes.
  • Composition has exclusive ownership.
  • Both association ends have been read aloud.
  • Visibility is a decision, not decoration.
  • A reader can see the structure without a tour.

Ask the room

Questions that keep the model honest

  • Is this a class, an object, or only a value?
  • Which class owns this information?
  • What responsibility justifies this operation?
  • Is the relationship really is-a?
  • Could this part belong to two wholes at once?
  • What does the far multiplicity say?
  • Is this domain structure, or implementation structure?
  • Would a plain association be more precise?

Exercise 4 · find the problem

What is wrong with each claim?

  1. Customer is the general class of Order.
  2. Order is composed of Product, even though many orders share a product.
  3. Every customer has exactly one order.
  4. Employee stores departmentId, but the view must explain department membership.

Answers are on the next slide.

Suggested answer

Exercise 4

  1. An order is not a kind of customer. That generalization fails the is-a test.
  2. A product exists independently and can be referenced by many orders. Use an association.
  3. 1 to 1 blocks a customer with no orders and a customer with several.
  4. Model Department as a class and associate it when membership matters.

Exercise 5 · Library

Draw the class diagram

Member, Librarian, Book, BookCopy, Loan, Reservation.

  • A book describes a title and may have several physical copies.
  • A member may have zero or many loans.
  • Each loan concerns one book copy.
  • A copy is on at most one active loan at a time.
  • A member may reserve a book title.
  • Include one justified composition, and multiplicity where it matters.

Keep these twelve

  1. The diagram is static structure, not call order.
  2. A class is a type. An object is one instance.
  3. Compartments hold the name, attributes, and operations.
  4. Visibility is an access constraint.
  5. Generalization is a real is-a relationship.
  6. An abstract class is not instantiated.
  7. Association is the default structural line.
  8. Shared aggregation is weak. Use it sparingly.
  9. Composition needs exclusive ownership.
  10. Read multiplicity at both ends.
  11. Navigability and multiplicity are different questions.
  12. Keep the view scoped and traceable to the requirements.
